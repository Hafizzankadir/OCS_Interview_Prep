Each question in the deck is tagged with a recommended framework. The specific label may vary slightly by question (e.g. "STAR, ownership-focused"), but it always maps back to one of the core structures below. Use these as skeletons, not scripts — fill them with your own real details.

Reference: The Biographical Deep-Dive

Not in the random deck — this is a chronological walk-through, not single-shot Q&A
Some panels (especially FIB-style interviews) move stage-by-stage through your entire life history rather than asking isolated questions. Expect them to fill in your actual details at each stage below, in order. Rehearse a 20–30 second summary for each row, ready to expand if probed.
SCHOOLING — schools attended, types, reasons for any move, dates, distance/travel, effect on activities. EXAMS — types taken, subjects, results, effort, how holidays affected revision, further academic plans. INSIDE SCHOOL — prefect/monitor roles, sports and level played, clubs/societies, uniformed organisations, adventurous activities, trips. OUTSIDE INTERESTS (school years) — sports centre use, youth organisations (join/leave dates, rank, responsibilities, camps), hobbies, part-time jobs, holidays, career ambitions at the time. HIGHER EDUCATION — institution and why, course and dates, academic performance, living/finances, involvement in student life, sports/clubs, trips. EMPLOYMENT — jobs held, dates, responsibilities, reasons for leaving, current pay, further training, travel, social/community involvement. MANDATORY DISCLOSURE — any adverse police contact, offences, court judgements — answer honestly and factually. REFLECTION — the high point and low point of your life so far.

Reference: Essay Defence / Pressure Questions

Not in the random deck — these depend on your own submitted essay
Some panels probe your written application essay directly, in a more adversarial, rapid-fire tone. This tests composure under pointed questioning as much as knowledge. The pattern below is what to prepare for, regardless of the exact wording used on you.
1. Own any factual mistake in your essay calmly — explain why it happened, don't get flustered defending it. 2. For any skill or experience you claimed (IT, logistics, combat sports, etc.) — be ready to honestly state its real scope and how you'd bridge the gap to a military context. 3. For any document you cited (e.g. Defence White Paper) — know specific sections or points, not just the title. 4. For any big claim ("I'll modernise X", "optimise Y") — have one concrete, proportionate first step ready, not a grand promise. 5. If challenged on a personal result (fitness test, English test, etc.) — answer honestly and briefly, then pivot to what you're doing about it. 6. Stay calm and non-defensive throughout — the panel is often testing your composure under pressure as much as your answer's content.
"You're right that my essay could have been more specific there — what I meant was X, and here's the concrete first step I'd actually take rather than the broader claim I made in writing."
